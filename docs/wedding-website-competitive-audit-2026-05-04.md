# Wedding Website Competitive Audit

_Date:_ 2026-05-04
_Purpose:_ Capture the current wedding website/planning competitor landscape so DayOf can borrow the strongest product patterns without becoming generic.
_Method:_ Public source research from official product pages, help docs, app-store listings, and current comparison guides. No account-gated screenshots were captured in this pass.

## Scope

This is not literally every small wedding template site on the internet. It is the serious landscape DayOf should care about:

- Mass-market free wedding website platforms: The Knot, WeddingWire, Hitched, Zola, Joy, Minted.
- Premium design-led platforms: Riley & Grey, Bliss & Bone, WeddingWoo.
- Wedding planning suites: WedSites, Appy Couple, Say I do, GettingMarried, Amorly, Kaiplan, Bridely.ai.
- General site builders used for weddings: Squarespace, Wix, Canva.
- Invitation-first products: Greenvelope, Paperless Post, Basic Invite, Forevite, Online-RSVP, Invitatis, Gather With Us.
- Registry-first products with website/RSVP overlap: Honeyfund, Hitchd, MyRegistry.
- Photo/guest-memory products: Wedibox, GUESTPIX.
- Destination/AI entrants: Aisle, Bridely.ai.

## Executive Read

The wedding website market has converged around the same baseline: template site, RSVP, registry, guest list, schedule, travel, FAQs, password/private access, and matching stationery or QR sharing. Those are now table stakes.

The places competitors still differentiate:

1. Guest personalization:
   Riley & Grey, Joy, Appy Couple, Say I do, WedSites, GettingMarried, and Aisle all lean into showing different events or schedules to different guests. This matters more than template count because it prevents awkward visibility problems.

2. RSVP intelligence:
   Strong products do not just collect yes/no. They collect meal choices, dietary needs, song requests, plus-ones, children, accommodations, per-event attendance, and custom questions, then feed that into guest list, seating, catering, and reminders.

3. Guest communication:
   The best communication UX looks like delivery-state management, not a blank email composer. Greenvelope and Paperless Post are especially good models: sent, opened, viewed, responded, bounced, reminded, and segmented follow-ups.

4. Design confidence:
   Minted, Riley & Grey, Bliss & Bone, Basic Invite, and Greenvelope win by making couples feel visually safe. They sell "this will look like a real wedding object," not "build a website."

5. Day-of and after-wedding memory capture:
   Joy, Appy Couple, Wedibox, and GUESTPIX prove that photo sharing, live slideshows, guestbooks, and voice/video messages are a real adjacent wedge. QR-first, no-app upload is the strongest UX.

6. Planning workspace:
   WedSites, GettingMarried, Hitched, Amorly, Kaiplan, and Bridely.ai point toward a product line beyond the public website: budget, vendors, seating, checklist, timeline, reminders, and collaborator roles.

7. AI setup is emerging:
   Aisle and Bridely.ai explicitly market AI setup/planning. Most incumbents do not. DayOf can own "AI makes the wedding site and planning layer feel finished, not auto-generated" if the output stays honest and controllable.

## Competitor Notes

### The Knot

Positioning: The default incumbent. Free wedding website plus registry, guest list, vendor marketplace, checklist, budget, app, and stationery.

Feature notes:
- Free wedding website with a large design library, matching stationery, custom/premade URLs, RSVP, privacy controls, registry integration, hotel blocks, app editing, guest reminders, address collection, and vendor directory.
- Guest list and RSVP tools include spreadsheet import, address collection, events, questions, meal choices, privacy preferences, response tracking, reminders, and export.
- Private events are hidden from the public site and shown only inside the RSVP flow for invited guests.

UX/UI notes:
- Strong guided setup and "start here" flows.
- Broad ecosystem helps couples feel they are not missing a step.
- The product can feel marketplace-heavy and vendor-ad adjacent. Vendor marketplace trust issues are a known weakness in perception.

Pros:
- Comprehensive, free, familiar, and hard to beat on ecosystem.
- Registry and RSVPs are connected enough for normal couples.
- App and reminders reduce admin overhead.

Cons / gaps:
- Less premium or intimate visually than boutique competitors.
- Marketplace incentives can make the experience feel noisy.
- Private event details appear constrained to RSVP context rather than rich personalized portals.

DayOf takeaways:
- Match or beat the "everything is connected" perception.
- Avoid marketplace clutter. DayOf can feel calmer and more trust-first.
- Make private event visibility richer than a hidden RSVP box.

Sources: The Knot wedding websites and comparison guide; The Knot guest list and RSVP help.

### WeddingWire / Hitched

Positioning: The Knot Worldwide sibling brands. Free wedding website, guest manager, RSVPs, registry/gift list links, vendor discovery, checklist, budget, seating.

Feature notes:
- WeddingWire emphasizes website, registry, RSVP syncing with Guest List, accommodations, vendor marketplace, and app.
- Hitched UK emphasizes guest manager, RSVPs, seating chart, budget planner, checklist, supplier/venue discovery, Wedshoots album link, and free planning tools.

UX/UI notes:
- Very practical, checklist-oriented, marketplace-friendly.
- Hitched has a useful desktop/mobile preview toggle in its website creation flow.

Pros:
- Free tools and strong vendor/planning ecosystem.
- Good for couples who want to organize without paying.

Cons / gaps:
- Similar marketplace-noise risk as The Knot.
- The site builder can feel like a utility inside a larger vendor platform.

DayOf takeaways:
- Add obvious desktop/mobile preview everywhere public-site content is edited.
- Keep DayOf's owner dashboard more emotionally calm than directory-led tools.

Sources: WeddingWire wedding website and guest list docs; Hitched planner and website pages.

### Zola

Positioning: Registry-first all-in-one platform. Free wedding websites, registry, RSVP, guest list, matching paper.

Feature notes:
- Free site with hundreds of designs, matching invitations, custom URL/domain, password protection, RSVP, registry, guest list, travel, schedule, FAQs, video/custom touches.
- Registry is the strongest native commerce experience: shop from the website without link-outs, cash funds, universal add-from-any-store, group gifts, shipment timing control, exchanges before shipping, thank-you manager, and advisor support.
- RSVP is tied to guest list and events, supports meal preferences and custom questions.

UX/UI notes:
- Zola reduces choice anxiety with a simple "website + registry + paper" story.
- Registry commerce is guest-friendly because it keeps the purchase inside the Zola surface.

Pros:
- Best registry depth.
- Good all-in-one owner workflow.
- Matching paper is a clear buying path.

Cons / gaps:
- Registry business model can dominate the wedding site experience.
- Visual uniqueness may be weaker than boutique platforms.

DayOf takeaways:
- If DayOf does not build native registry commerce, the registry UX should still feel intentional: clear external registry cards, cash fund language, purchased/claimed state, thank-you tracking, and no ugly link farm.
- DayOf should not try to out-Zola Zola on commerce right now.

Sources: Zola wedding website, Zola registry, Zola RSVP FAQ.

### Joy

Positioning: Guest-experience led, free website and app with registry, RSVPs, guest list, travel, schedules, photos.

Feature notes:
- Wedding website, guest list, RSVPs, invitations, registry, accommodations, mobile app.
- Personalized schedules, travel details, map integrations, accommodations booking, ride-share integrations, guest photo collection, virtual guestbook, live slideshow, app-based guest experience.
- Guest list includes plus-ones, families/groups, tags, multiple editors, spreadsheet import/export, messages/cards, RSVP tracking, invite open tracking, gift purchase tracking.

UX/UI notes:
- Joy's edge is "all wedding details wherever guests go."
- The app makes the wedding site feel alive on the day, but guests can still use the web.
- Guest photo sharing and schedule access are natural day-of use cases.

Pros:
- Best model for guest app/day-of companion.
- Strong personalized guest schedule and photo collection story.
- Lower visual clutter than marketplace-led platforms.

Cons / gaps:
- App can be a barrier if guests are asked to download it.
- Some reviewers note fewer heavy planning tools like budget/checklist.

DayOf takeaways:
- DayOf should push "guest mode" and "coordinator mode" as live event companions, without forcing app download.
- Add a clearer owner/guest mental model around personalized schedules, ride/travel, and photo moments.

Sources: Joy guest list, Joy app, App Store and Google Play listings.

### Minted

Positioning: Stationery-first wedding website. Best for couples who want the website to match invitations and paper.

Feature notes:
- Free websites, hundreds of artist-made designs, matching stationery, guest list, RSVPs for all wedding events, password protection, registry links, custom URL upgrade, QR code/URL on stationery, $50 stationery credit.
- Guest RSVP is name-search based and visually simple.

UX/UI notes:
- Minted sells taste and visual legitimacy. The product copy talks less about planning complexity and more about seamless guest navigation and matching paper.
- The site is strongest before the wedding, as part of the invitation suite.

Pros:
- Strong trust through independent artist designs and matching print.
- Good for couples anxious about aesthetics.

Cons / gaps:
- Weaker full planning ecosystem.
- Registry is link-out, not native.

DayOf takeaways:
- DayOf templates need stronger "wedding object" credibility: matching QR cards, invite cards, signage, and stationery-style preview exports.
- Add "guest URL/QR card" assets as a first-class output.

Sources: Minted wedding website page.

### WedSites

Positioning: Planning-suite product with website, guest list, RSVPs, paperless stationery, seating, budget, timeline, multilingual.

Feature notes:
- Drag-and-drop website builder, single-page or multi-page layouts, custom domain forwarding, registry links, guestbook, travel/accommodations, wedding party bios, visual event timeline, countdown, livestream links, custom calligraphy, publishing control, password, analytics, custom CSS.
- Guest manager includes households, groups, plus-ones, A/B lists, notes, RSVP statuses, stationery tracking, printable exports.
- RSVP builder supports multi-event RSVP and custom questionnaires.
- Pro includes checklist, seating chart, reports, timeline/runsheet, budget, vendor payment reminders, multilingual website and invitations, multiple user accounts.

UX/UI notes:
- Very feature-complete.
- The feature taxonomy is excellent: website, designs, guest list, RSVPs, paperless stationery, planning dashboard.
- Multilingual is handled as duplicated/translated guest experience, not just machine translation.

Pros:
- Deepest planning-suite blueprint outside the incumbents.
- Multilingual and A/B list are strong differentiators.

Cons / gaps:
- Paid tiers for many advanced functions.
- Could feel more complex than a couple wants if the UI is not staged well.

DayOf takeaways:
- DayOf should copy the staged feature taxonomy, but keep default views lighter.
- High-value gaps for DayOf: A/B guest list, visitor analytics, multilingual per-guest language, RSVP printable catering reports, vendor payment reminders.

Sources: WedSites features and multilingual pages.

### Appy Couple

Feature notes:
- Website and app stay synced.
- Flexible sections, layout switching, unlimited guests, RSVP manager, custom RSVP forms, plus-ones, family groups, multiple event privacy, access control, guest list match, password, custom sections, photo sharing, guestbook, countdown, travel concierge, registry/gifting, address collection, email communications, notifications, multiple admins, multilingual content, AirPlay/Miracast photo streaming.

Pros:

Cons / gaps:

DayOf takeaways:

- Add live photo wall/slideshow and "virtual guestbook/toast" style moments to DayOf's photo/vault surfaces.

Sources: Appy Couple features and App Store listing.

### Say I do

Positioning: Free and paid RSVP wedding website with guest visibility controls, QR, digital invitations, checklist.

Feature notes:
- 100+ templates, events, photos/videos, timeline, accommodation, maps/directions, countdown, password, custom domain, smart QR codes, guest list, online RSVP, private sections/events, single or multi-page layouts, digital save-the-dates/invitations, checklist.
- Help docs expose nuanced forms/questions, per-event RSVP questions, guest-specific visibility, language changes, domains, QR codes.

UX/UI notes:
- The promise is pragmatic: get RSVPs and guest list working fast.
- "Choose what guests see" is a prominent differentiator.

Pros:
- Strong guest visibility and QR flow.

Cons / gaps:

DayOf takeaways:
- Bring QR into every relevant output: RSVP, photo upload, guestbook, website, table signs.
- Make private event/section visibility easier to understand in the builder.

Sources: Say I do wedding website and help center.

### GettingMarried

Positioning: UK free wedding website plus paid guest management, email stationery, cash gift list, menu, table planner.

Feature notes:
- Free tier up to 75 guests with website, guest list, simple RSVP.
- Paid tiers add premium designs, personal address, QR, email guests, automated RSVP, unlimited email stationery, tailored guest questions, menu manager, table planner.
- RSVP methods include simple contact form, automated RSVP, email address identification, guest group passwords, open access, unique RSVP codes.
- Static site may show all public event info, but RSVP guest area shows only assigned events.

UX/UI notes:
- Very practical, UK-specific, direct.
- Unique RSVP code explanation is useful because guests understand "password" better than "code."
- Menu manager and table planner are tightly coupled to guest list.

Pros:
- Strong value pricing.
- Excellent catering/menu/table planner pipeline.
- Thoughtful RSVP access options.

Cons / gaps:
- Static website visibility model can leak too much context unless owners understand guest areas.
- Visual design appears less premium than newer design-led entrants.

DayOf takeaways:
- Add RSVP access method options: named lookup, private household link, event/group password, unique code.
- Make catering exports and table-plan handoff extremely strong.

Sources: GettingMarried features, pricing, RSVP help, how-it-works.

### Smaller Invitation/RSVP Website Products

These are not as strategically important as The Knot, Zola, Joy, Minted, Riley & Grey, WedSites, or Appy Couple, but they expose useful launch UX patterns.

#### Forevite

Positioning: Animated digital wedding invitation with RSVP, guest list, seating, multilingual, and wedding website in one link.

Feature notes:
- 12+ animated themes, smart RSVP, dietary needs, song requests, companions, visual seating planner, visual content builder, dashboard, multilingual invitations, QR codes, background music, one-time pricing, agency multi-client mode.

UX/UI notes:
- Strong demo-account pitch: prospects can request a preloaded premium account with sample guests and seating plan.
- Explicit comparison table against Joy, Greenvelope, and Paperless Post frames it as one-time event pricing instead of subscription/per-send.

DayOf takeaways:
- A preloaded "demo wedding" workspace could help couples understand DayOf without touching their own data.
- One-time event pricing is emotionally cleaner than subscription framing for weddings.

#### Invitatis

Positioning: Fast multilingual wedding website/invitation with RSVP.

Feature notes:
- 1-minute setup, digital invitation, online RSVP, WhatsApp/Telegram/SMS/email/social sharing, plus-ones, dietary needs, comments, PDF/spreadsheet export, Spotify/YouTube embeds, countdown, calendar save, 7 languages, one all-inclusive publication fee.

UX/UI notes:
- The strongest pattern is "share via WhatsApp in one tap," because real wedding logistics often happen in messaging apps.
- Multilingual is guest-facing, not just site-owner translation.

- Add a WhatsApp/SMS/email/share-sheet first class share flow for RSVP and photo upload links.

#### Online-RSVP.com

Positioning: Older or regional website builders that still expose useful patterns around live editing, event-specific RSVP, and culturally specific sharing.

Feature notes:
- WeddingWoo focuses on visual customization, live preview editing, custom RSVP forms, guestbook, song requests, custom domain, password privacy, unlimited photos/bandwidth, widgets, and simple flat pricing.
- To-wed supports multiple events, event-specific invitations/questions, guest permission controls, email RSVP, CSV import, guest photo uploads, maps, registry/wishing well, maybe responses, and unlimited images.
- TheWeddingLink is positioned around Indian weddings with handcrafted templates, digital invitations, RSVP, schedule, gallery, studio delivery tools, WhatsApp sharing, and free couple websites.

UX/UI notes:
- Live preview editing is still a valuable confidence pattern.
- Event-specific visibility appears even in smaller products, which confirms it is a real table-stakes trust feature.
- WhatsApp sharing is especially important for Indian/global weddings and family-forward planning.

DayOf takeaways:
- Keep live preview fast and obvious.
- Do not bury multi-event privacy in advanced settings.
- Treat WhatsApp/share-sheet links as first-class in guest communication.

Sources: WeddingWoo, To-wed, TheWeddingLink.

## UX Patterns Worth Borrowing

### 1. Preview As Guest

Best models: Riley & Grey, Aisle, Say I do, Appy Couple.

Why it matters: Owners worry that rehearsal dinner details, lodging, plus-ones, or family-only events are visible to the wrong people. This is a trust issue, not a settings issue.

DayOf version:
- In builder/settings/RSVP, add a "Preview as..." control.
- Let owner pick guest, household, planner, coordinator, public visitor.
- Show a visibility banner: "Viewing as Maya Chen: invited to Ceremony, Reception, After Party."

### 2. One QR For The Wedding

Best models: Say I do, Wedibox, GUESTPIX, Honeyfund, Greenvelope.

Why it matters: Couples print once. Guests do not want multiple links.

DayOf version:
- One QR opens a guest hub that routes to RSVP, site, photo upload, guestbook, registry, directions.
- Owner can generate table cards, invitation inserts, welcome signs, bathroom/bar signs, and photo prompt cards.

### 3. RSVP Access Modes

Best models: GettingMarried, The Knot, Zola, Aisle.

Why it matters: Different couples need different privacy/friction tradeoffs.

DayOf version:
- Name lookup.
- Private household link.
- Guest code/password.
- Open RSVP for all.
- Phone/email verification for high-privacy weddings.
- Clear "recommended" default based on guest list state.

### 4. Status-Based Messaging

Best models: Greenvelope, Paperless Post, The Knot.

Why it matters: Couples do not want to compose messages. They want "remind people who have not RSVP'd."

DayOf version:
- Segments: not opened invite, opened not RSVP'd, yes no meal, attending no address, declined, bounced, delivery failed, needs review.
- Message templates with editable preview.
- Delivery states and safe retry logic.

### 5. Wedding Identity Kit

Best models: Minted, Bliss & Bone, Basic Invite, Greenvelope.

Why it matters: A website alone feels less valuable than a coherent wedding identity.

DayOf version:
- Site theme outputs: QR cards, detail insert, photo upload sign, RSVP card, table card, monogram/avatar, palette, type pair.
- Export as PNG/PDF for printing or texting.

### 6. No-App Day-Of Memory Capture

Best models: Wedibox, GUESTPIX, Joy.

Why it matters: Day-of guest participation must be almost effortless.

DayOf version:
- QR opens upload in browser.
- No guest login.
- Optional name capture.
- Album suggestions by event/time.
- Live slideshow.
- Full-res download.
- Owner moderation and private/public gallery toggles.

### 7. Gentle Planning Digest

Best model: Amorly.

Why it matters: Dashboards can become guilt machines. A digest is calmer.

DayOf version:
- Morning summary: new RSVPs, next payment/task, messages needing review, photo uploads, schedule changes, guest count, days remaining.
- Send to owners/planners only.

### 8. Destination Guest Portal

Best model: Aisle.

Why it matters: Destination weddings are where generic wedding sites collapse under travel details.

DayOf version:
- Per-guest portal with airport, hotel, room/block assignment, shuttle, schedule, dress code, RSVP, FAQ, local recs.
- Use AI setup to draft travel guide, but keep owner review required.

## Product Thesis For DayOf

DayOf should not try to be "The Knot plus prettier templates." The better lane is:

> A calm, wedding-native operating system where the site, guest list, RSVP, photos, messages, seating, coordinator flow, and AI setup all know about each other.

The market gives us a clear opening:

- Incumbents are broad but noisy.
- Premium builders are beautiful but incomplete.
- Photo tools are delightful but narrow.
- Planning workspaces are practical but often visually thin.
- AI entrants are early.

DayOf can win by feeling more human than the incumbents, more operational than the design boutiques, more complete than the QR/photo tools, and more trustworthy than generic AI planning apps.
