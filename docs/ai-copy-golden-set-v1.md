# AI copy golden set v1

Built from manually reviewed text pulls in `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/`.

## Target voice
- first person when it helps
- practical with emotional undertone
- warm but not mushy
- helpful without sounding like support copy
- not corporate
- not brochure / concierge / etiquette-blog voice

---

## Story / our story

### Good patterns
**Why they work:** concrete, human, chronological, easy to read.

- `We met at the Library in San Luis Obispo, CA. Not the library you read books but the library you dance and enjoy a night out with your friends :)`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/zola_gustavoandcourtney.txt`
- `In March of 2020, at Texas A&M University... Little did he know that this nearly missed opportunity would lead him to the love of his life.`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/zola_mirandnoah2026.txt`
- `Our journey began in a way I never expected — at a wedding!`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/zola_kariahandaric.txt`

### What to emulate
- starts with a concrete moment
- uses real places / dates / situations when available
- sounds like a person telling a story, not summarizing a resume
- first person is strongest when source info supports it

### Bad patterns
- `Now they can't wait to spend the rest of their lives together.`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/brownkidderwedding.txt`
- `We knew we wanted to spend the rest of our lives with one another from the very start.`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/zola_gustavoandcourtney.txt`

### Avoid
- “rest of our lives” filler
- destiny/soulmate shortcuts when no concrete story exists
- third-person recap when first person would feel more human

## Travel / getting here

### Good patterns
**Why they work:** direct, helpful, location-forward.

- `Pack your bags, We’re going to Mexico!`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/stefandphil.txt`
- `Please join us for a glamping-style weekend wedding retreat in the Santa Cruz Mountains.`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/shawnandtara2025.txt`
- `Join us in Tuscany for a weekend of celebration.`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/mannion_wedding.txt`

### What to emulate
- lead with place + vibe quickly
- travel copy should orient, not market the destination
- one helpful concrete line beats three polished vague ones

### Bad patterns
- `Need a place to stay? Look no further.`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/zola_gustavoandcourtney.txt`

### Avoid
- tourism-board phrasing
- “local planning help” / “arrival details” / “helpful information” sludge
- hospitality tone

## FAQ intro / guest-help framing

### Good patterns
**Why they work:** direct and useful.

- `You have questions, we have answers`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/claudiaandmarijn.txt`
- `This site has everything you’ll need leading up to the big day— event details, travel and hotel info, FAQs, a guide to CDMX...`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/stefandphil.txt`

### What to emulate
- simple utility framing is okay
- direct is better than elegant here
- guest-help sections can be plainer than story sections

### Bad patterns
- generic AI-ish lines like `Answers to common questions` or `A few helpful notes`
  - not from source sites; identified as weak generated outputs

### Avoid
- support-center language
- corporate helper tone
- vague “helpful details” with no texture

## Registry

### Good patterns
**Why they work:** direct, grateful, not too long.

- `Many of you are traveling across the globe to celebrate with us in Italy, so your presence means the world to us and is all we ask for.`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/claudiaandmarijn.txt`
- `Your presence and prayers on our big day are all we ask for! However, should you wish to help us celebrate with a gift...`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/alexandbailey2024.txt`

### What to emulate
- acknowledge presence first
- keep it short
- if gifts are mentioned, make it feel optional and calm

### Bad patterns
- `Your presence is the greatest gift`
  - common template phrase; too canned
- `we appreciate your thoughtfulness`
  - etiquette-blog tone

### Avoid
- gift-poem voice
- apologetic registry language
- stiff gratitude phrasing

---

## RSVP / CTA

### Good patterns
**Why they work:** short, direct, human.

- `Will we see you in Italy? Let’s make it official :)`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/claudiaandmarijn.txt`
- `don’t forget to send that in by February 15th!`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/stefandphil.txt`
- `We hope you can celebrate with us!`
  - Source: `tmp/ai-copy-source-text/shawnandtara2025.txt`

### What to emulate
- direct ask
- light personality is okay
- first person helps
- avoid legalistic / formal tone

### Bad patterns
- `Kindly confirm your attendance`
  - weak generated pattern
- `Please let us know if you’ll join us by...`
  - acceptable but often too generic

### Avoid
- “kindly”
- “attendance”
- formal invitation-office phrasing

## Wedding party intro

### Good patterns
The source set is weak here. Most sites rely on labels/lists, not good intro copy.

### Practical conclusion
This section should probably be:
- very short
- first person if possible
- more grounded than sentimental

### Avoid
- `The people who mean the most to us`
- `standing with us on this special day`
- `we’re pleased to introduce`

---

## Global anti-patterns from reviewed sources + generated outputs
Avoid these families hard:
- soulmate / forever shortcuts
- “rest of our lives” filler
- “special day”
- “journey”
- “those we hold dear”
- “closest friends and family” when it adds nothing
- corporate helper phrasing
- hotel / concierge tone
- etiquette-blog registry voice

---

## Positive writing rules
1. start concrete when possible
2. use first person for emotional body copy
3. let guest-help sections be plain and useful
4. one clear sentence beats two elegant vague ones
5. practical + warm wins over poetic + generic
