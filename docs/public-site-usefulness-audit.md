# Public Site Usefulness Audit

Date: 2026-04-13

## Current real strengths
- Public-site copy is calmer, more tasteful, and less generic than before.
- Travel, schedule, FAQ, venue, registry, and RSVP sections are materially better than the old defaults.
- First-draft generation is less likely to ship weak empty sections.

## Where usefulness still feels thin for serious couples

### 1. Some sections are still better at presentation than decision support
The site can look good and explain basics, but serious couples also want guests to feel:
- fully oriented
- less likely to text basic questions
- more confident about logistics

Thin spots still tend to show up when a guest needs exact practical confidence, not just pretty layout.

### 2. Multi-event and weekend complexity still risks feeling flatter than real weddings
DayOf has made progress here, but the public site can still feel too simplified when a wedding includes:
- welcome drinks
- ceremony + reception split details
- brunch / farewell plans
- shuttle timing
- partial attendance expectations

### 3. Household-specific usefulness is still limited on the public side
The ops layer is getting smarter, but the guest-facing site still does not always feel deeply aware of:
- who this guest is in the household
- what exactly applies to them
- what they still need to do

### 4. Trustful “what happens next” guidance can still improve
Serious couples want guests to know:
- whether they are done
- whether they still owe an RSVP detail
- where to look for updates later
- how day-of changes will be communicated

## Product-safe truth
Safe claim:
- DayOf gives guests a calmer, more useful wedding site with stronger logistics and RSVP support than a simple brochure page.

Unsafe claim:
- DayOf already delivers deeply personalized guest-specific public guidance across every wedding scenario.

## Recommended next moves
1. Strengthen practical confidence cues on public sections
2. Improve multi-event / weekend guest clarity
3. Surface better “what happens next” guidance after RSVP and on return visits
4. Keep claims grounded in useful logistics support, not overpersonalized guest intelligence
